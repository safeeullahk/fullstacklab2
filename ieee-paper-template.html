I. Introduction

Introduce the research problem and explain why it matters. Give readers enough context to understand the topic, then state the specific gap this work addresses. Keep the opening focused and support claims with appropriate references.

This paper makes the following contributions: (1) describe the first contribution; (2) explain the second contribution; and (3) summarize the evaluation and its main findings. Close this section with a short guide to the rest of the paper.

II. Related Work

Summarize the most relevant prior work and group it by approach or theme. Explain how those studies relate to the present work. Cite sources consistently, for example, [1], [2]. Avoid listing papers without describing their connection to the research question.

A. Background

Define important terms and assumptions that will be used throughout the paper. Keep this subsection focused on information needed to understand the proposed method.

III. Proposed Method

Describe the system, model, or procedure in enough detail for another researcher to understand and reproduce it. Introduce variables before using them and explain each stage of the method.

V. Results and Discussion

Present the key findings in a clear order, referring to figures and tables in the text. Explain what the results show, compare them with suitable baselines, and discuss unexpected outcomes. Report measurements accurately and avoid claims not supported by the evidence.

VI. Conclusion

Restate the problem and summarize the main contribution and findings. Mention important limitations and identify a concrete direction for future work. Do not introduce new results in the conclusion.
